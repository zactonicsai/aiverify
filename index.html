Key Legal Standards for AI Evidence

Federal Rules of Evidence

Rule 901(a): Requires that evidence be authenticated — the proponent must produce evidence sufficient to support a finding that the item is what they claim.

Rule 901(b)(9): Allows authentication through evidence describing a process or system and showing it produces an accurate result.

Rule 702 (Daubert): Expert testimony on AI detection must be based on sufficient facts, reliable principles, and reliable application.

Emerging Standards

C2PA / Content Credentials: Cryptographic provenance metadata embedded in media files at creation.

NIST AI 100-4: Framework for reducing risks of synthetic content, including detection benchmarks.

ABA Resolution 604 (2024): Urging courts to develop protocols for AI-generated evidence.

⚠️ Common Pitfalls

✕Relying on a single AI detection tool — different tools have different false-positive rates
✕Failing to preserve the original file (compression/conversion destroys metadata)
✕Not documenting the chain of custody for digital evidence
✕Accepting screenshots as primary evidence without verifying the source
✕Ignoring EXIF/metadata inconsistencies in photos and documents
✕Treating AI detection confidence scores as definitive proof

✓ Best Practices

✓Use at least 3 independent AI detection tools and cross-reference results
✓Always obtain and preserve the original native file format
✓Establish and document digital chain of custody with timestamps and hashes
✓Engage a qualified digital forensics expert for high-stakes cases
✓Check for C2PA content credentials and provenance data
✓Document every step of the verification process for court admissibility
